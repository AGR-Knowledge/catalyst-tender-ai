C O N T E X T
Tendering is where EPC contractors win or lose the year
Every major bid is a compressed, document-heavy, multi-party programme run against an immovable deadline.
A single ITT can arrive as hundreds of pages across specifications,
drawings, forms and addenda. From that, a bid team must extract
every obligation, package the scope, price dozens of subcontract
trades, build a credible programme, draft a persuasive proposal
and evidence total compliance — usually in three to six weeks,
alongside every other live bid.
The work is done well by experienced people. It is simply done
manually, in parallel, under time pressure, and largely without
institutional memory.
Fragmented intake
Portals, inboxes and hard copy
monitored inconsistently — tenders
found late or missed entirely

Nine points of failure across the tender lifecycle
Mapped to the nine stages every EPC bid actually passes through.
1 Identification & screening
Portals watched inconsistently; tenders surface late,
leaving no time to shape the bid.
2 Subcontractor inputs
Quotes arrive in incomparable formats, late, or not at
all; coverage is thin on key packages.
3 Bid / No-Bid
Pursuit decided on anecdote and optimism; weak bids
consume the resource strong bids needed.
4 Scheduling & planning
Programme built in isolation from cost; it stops
reflecting reality the moment scope moves.
5 Cost & margin modelling
Overheads, bonds, escalation and FX treated
inconsistently between bids and estimators.
6 Proposal drafting
Content re-invented from scratch; what won last time
is not found, so it is not reused.
7 Compliance verification
The matrix is assembled last, under pressure, so gaps
are found far too late to close well.
8 Compilation & submission
Formatting, signatures and portal constraints become a
deadline-night risk to the whole bid.
9 Post-award & learning
Outcomes are recorded but never analysed; the next
bid starts no wiser than the last.

P A I N P O I N T 0 2
Margin is decided in spreadsheets nobody can reconstruct
The commercial heart of the bid is the least governed part of the process.
Inconsistent build-ups
Every estimator layers overheads, preliminaries, insurances and bonds slightly differently. Two bids
for similar work carry different embedded margin, and nobody can see it.
Static assumptions
FX, escalation and material exposure are fixed at a point in time. When a supplier re-quotes in week
four, the model is not re-run — it is patched.
No scenario view
One number goes to the review board. There is no base, stretch and defensive case, no sensitivity,
and no explicit break-even.
Instinct-led pricing
The final margin call is made on experience and pressure rather than on evidence of what actually
wins at what price.
The compounding effect
Because the cost model cannot be
reconstructed, it cannot be challenged — and
because it is never compared against as-built
outcomes, it never improves.
The result is a pricing function that carries the
largest commercial risk in the business while
generating the least evidence about its own
accuracy.
